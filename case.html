The Bridge: Freedmen to Native

Connection Case BR-0001 (sample)

One family, two record worlds. On the left, a federal census household that records the Reeds as a Black farming family. On the right, a Dawes enrollment card that records the same people as citizens of the Choctaw Nation. The case asks a single question: are these the same household?

Confidence: Probable

States never blur. Documented means the person stands in Native side records in their own right and connects into the tree. Connected means the line runs through a documented person. Probable means a strong match with one gap left. Lead means worth chasing. This case sits at Probable, and below you can see exactly why, and exactly what would close it.

The matching signals

6 of 6 household members align across the two records, in the same order, two years apart.

  • ✓Head and spouse match exactly. Solomon and Adeline Reed head both households, with no competing Reed household of this shape in the county on either side.
  • ✓The children match as a cluster. Four children, same names, same birth order, on both sides. The engine matches households, not just names, because households tell the truth.
  • ✓The age math holds. Every member ages two to three years across the two year gap between records. Nobody ages backwards, nobody appears from nowhere.
  • ✓The ground matches. The census township on Side A sits inside the allotment district named on Side B. The family did not move; the paperwork did.
  • ✓The classification flip points the right way. Side A records the family as Black; Side B enrolls them as Choctaw Freedmen citizens. For families split by the rolls, that flip is the fingerprint of the split, and the engine counts it as supporting evidence.
  • !One honest mismatch. The daughter is Bettie, 16, on Side A and Elizabeth, 18, on Side B. Bettie is a period nickname for Elizabeth, and one year of age drift is common in these records. The engine flags the mismatch on the case instead of smoothing it over, and it is part of why this case is Probable and not Documented.

What would prove this

  • The full Dawes enrollment jacket. The sworn testimony inside it usually names parents, residence history, and how long the family lived in the Nation. Testimony matching the census ground would close the Bettie and Elizabeth gap.
  • The 1885 Choctaw census household. The same family group fifteen years earlier, in the same county, would anchor the cluster before either of these records existed.
  • A Freedman's Bank register entry for Solomon or Adeline. Bank registers name parents, siblings, and spouses, and one entry could settle the family shape on the Freedmen side for good.

Every one of these is a public record, and all three already sit in the Our LGCY reference corpus or on its pull list. No cousin has to find them in a drawer.

The rule the case lives by

The app presents evidence. It does not enroll anybody. Citizenship in a tribal nation is decided by that nation under its own law, and nothing on this page confers, confirms, or restores it.

What the app does is the research: both record worlds in one graph, the signals laid out where the family can read them, and the gaps named in plain language so the next cousin knows exactly what to hunt for.
