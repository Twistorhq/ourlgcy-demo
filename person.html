History book page, sample ancestor

Solomon Reed

Born enslaved in the Choctaw Nation, freed as a boy, married at twenty two, counted in two nations' records, and enrolled a citizen of the Choctaw Nation on the Freedmen roll. He farmed the same county for half a century and died a Mason, with his lodge brothers carrying him to the grave.

Born
c. 1852, Choctaw Nation, Indian Territory
Died
1931, Choctaw County, Oklahoma
Enrolled
Choctaw Freedmen, 1902
Buried
Reed Family Cemetery (sample)

How to read this page: every fact carries its source. Green chips are public records already inside Our LGCY. Red chips are records the family itself supplied. When the record is silent, the page says so, plainly.

His life, in order

  1. c. 1852

    Born in the Choctaw Nation, Indian Territory. His age is given as 48 in the 1900 census, which sets his birth near 1852. The census taker recorded his birthplace as Indian Territory.

    1900 US Census, Indian Territory (sample)
  2. 1866

    Freed under the treaty of 1866. In his enrollment testimony decades later, Solomon said he was "born a slave in the Choctaw Nation and made free by the treaty." The testimony is the source; the words are quoted, not paraphrased.

    Dawes application testimony (sample)
  3. 1874

    Marries Adeline Carter. The Bureau register records the marriage of Solomon Reed and Adeline Carter and names the minister. Her maiden name, Carter, is the thread that connects her own line.

    Freedmen's Bureau marriage register (sample)
  4. 1885

    Counted in the Choctaw census. The tribal census lists Solomon, Adeline, and three children in Kiamitia County, fifteen years before the federal census taker finds the same household.

    Indian Census Rolls, 1885 (sample)
  5. 1900

    Farming his own place. The federal census lists a household of seven. Solomon's occupation reads "farmer," and the farm schedule marks the land as owned, not rented.

    1900 US Census (sample)
  6. 1902

    Enrolled as a citizen, Freedmen roll. The Dawes Commission approves Solomon, Adeline, and their minor children for enrollment as Choctaw Freedmen. The enrollment facts on this page are quoted from the roll exactly as it states them.

    Dawes Final Rolls (sample entry)
  7. 1907

    Allotment and statehood. Solomon receives his allotment in what becomes Choctaw County, Oklahoma, the same ground his family had farmed for decades. The root couple's branch of the tree anchors here.

    Allotment record (sample)
  8. 1930

    The last census. At about seventy eight, Solomon is recorded in the household of his son James, occupation "none," the census taker's blunt word for a man who had earned his rest.

    1930 US Census (sample)
  9. 1931

    Dies, and his lodge carries him home. His funeral program names him "a proud member" of his Prince Hall lodge and lists the brothers who served as pallbearers. The program is one page, and it proves more than some archives do.

    Funeral program, 1931 (sample, family supplied)

His standing

  • Prince Hall Masons: member, Harmony Lodge (sample). The funeral program names membership only, so this page says member, not officer.
  • Church: member, Mt. Zion Baptist Church (sample), named in the same program.
  • Community: head of household in four federal censuses and one tribal census, a span of forty five years on the same ground.

What the record does not say

The testimony names his mother, June. His father's name appears in no record found yet, and this page will not guess at one.

What would fill the gap: the full Dawes enrollment jacket, the 1866 Choctaw Freedmen census, and a Freedman's Bank register for the county, if one survives.

His branch

Wife: Adeline Reed, born Carter. Children in the sample tree: Martha, James, Bettie, and Thomas, the four branches on the home page tree.
