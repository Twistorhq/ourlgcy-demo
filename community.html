A village of paper, kept in the present day

Community

The family steps off the tree and into a place of their own: a papercraft village street, built of layered paper, where the sample ancestors keep their rounds between the homeplace, the church, the store, and the well. The village runs on your hours, not theirs: come at midday and the street bustles; come at night and most everyone is home. Each villager stays the age they were when they passed, living that age in today's world, and now and then someone marvels at what another generation left on their street. Tap a villager to open their profile.

Who lives here

Dress, tools, transport, and dwellings follow documented years: each figure is drawn for the decade of their young adulthood, figured from the birth year on their tree card, and the village keeps to each era's way of getting there, horse and buggy for the oldest rounds, early motorcars, then sedans, then today's plain sport utility shapes, none of them branded. Where a record gives no year, a figure stays era-neutral. Four rounds cross the village: the homeplace round, the church round, the store round, and the road to town. The buildings are era-typical for the family's span, not tied to one person's record. The street itself lives in the current year and keeps your current time: its light, its crowds, and its traffic all follow the hour on your clock. And each ancestor is kept at the age they were when they passed: that age never advances, even as the village year does.
