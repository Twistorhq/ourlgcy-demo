The village under the oak

Community

Below the tree, the family lives. The sample ancestors keep a village day under the oak, each on their own round between the homeplace, the church, the store, and the well, getting there the way their years allowed. Tap a villager to open their profile.

Who lives here

Dress, tools, transport, and dwellings follow documented years: each figure is drawn for the decade of their young adulthood, figured from the birth year on their tree card, and the village keeps to each era's way of getting there, horse and buggy for the oldest rounds, early motorcars, then sedans, then today's plain sport utility shapes, none of them branded. Where a record gives no year, a figure stays era-neutral. Four rounds cross the village: the homeplace round, the church round, the store round, and the road to town. The buildings are era-typical for the family's span, not tied to one person's record.
